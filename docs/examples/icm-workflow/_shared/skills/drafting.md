# Skill — Drafting

## When to load
Stage 4, before writing `draft_v1.md`.

## Non-negotiables
- No new claims. If the outline does not have it, do not write it.
- Every factual sentence carries an inline citation `[Publisher](url)` sourced from `source_bibliography.md`.
- Anchor text must read naturally when links are stripped. Never use "source", "link", "here", or raw URLs as anchor text.
- Length lands within ±10 % of the scope target.

## Voice
- Sentences: prefer 15–22 words. Break anything over 30.
- Verbs: prefer active, concrete. Avoid "leverage", "unlock", "seamlessly".
- Hedging: match confidence. `LOW` claims → "some evidence suggests"; `HIGH` claims → declarative.

## Citation placement
- Cite at the end of the sentence that carries the claim, before the terminal period.
- One citation per sentence is the norm. Two is acceptable when the sentence combines two facts from two sources.
- Never bundle citations at the end of a paragraph.

## Common mistakes
- Padding to hit word count with adjectives.
- Silent scope creep: adding a "helpful" claim not in the outline.
- Attaching a citation to a sentence the source does not actually support (verify by round-tripping through the evidence log).
