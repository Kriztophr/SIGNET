# Airchelon — Business Model & Pricing Strategy

*Companion to the Airchelon Brand Strategy and Logo Brief. Modeled on Every.to's real subscription-bundle economics, adapted to Airchelon's regulated-industry-friendly, format-flexible positioning.*

---

## 1. What Every's numbers actually show

Before designing Airchelon's model, it's worth being precise about what Every has actually done — because the *sequence* matters as much as the destination.

- **Pricing today:** $30/month, or $288/year ($24/month effective) for the full membership — essays + archive, live workshops/camps, and the four-app software bundle (Cora, Monologue, Sparkle, Spiral) ([every.to/pricing](https://every.to/pricing), [every.to/faq](https://every.to/faq)).
- **A newer top tier ("All Access")** at $500 first year / $625/year renewal adds unlimited app usage, $7,000+ in partner AI-tool credits, and exclusive programming — a high-margin tier for the most engaged subscribers ([every.to/on-every/introducing-every-all-access](https://every.to/on-every/introducing-every-all-access)).
- **Price history:** 13 bundled newsletters for $200/year in 2020 → today's media + software bundle at $288/year — a 44% increase justified by adding the app bundle, not just raising the price on the same product ([Startup Spells](https://startupspells.com/p/every-to-defensible-subscription-bundle-media-software-creator-economy)).
- **Revenue mix (March 2026, ~13 months after launching consulting):** Subscriptions $250K/mo, Consulting $215K/mo, Courses $77K/mo, Software $63K/mo — roughly $605K/month, or ~$7.3M annualized ([LinkedIn / Joe Daniels](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/uptojoegood_i-just-saw-a-great-breakdown-of-every-incs-activity-7451962955983798272-VO-_)).
- **The critical detail:** consulting launched in February 2025 and within 13 months was generating almost as much monthly revenue as subscriptions — a business line built over five years. It's now ~35% of total revenue and still growing fastest.
- **2025 total revenue was ~$3M** (3x growth that year); the team's own 2026 target is roughly another 3x, toward ~$6M ARR / ~$10M total revenue ([Every's Master Plan video, Jan 2026](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aBQ3MK4tvKQ)).
- **Writer compensation** is revenue-share, not salary: writers get a guaranteed minimum per piece plus ~50% of the subscription revenue attributed to them, measured via "marginal churn contribution" — subscribers are asked which writer's work would make them cancel if that writer left ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2021/01/26/everything-bundle-substack-newsletters), [Every: Am I getting ripped off writing at Every?](https://every.to/napkin-math/am-i-getting-ripped-off-writing-at-every)).

**The takeaway for Airchelon:** software took Every years to build and monetize meaningfully (~10% of revenue even now); consulting, which required no product at all, overtook years of subscription-building in about a year. For a brand explicitly positioned around regulated industries and AI governance — audiences with real training and advisory budgets — that suggests a different build order than Every's own.

## 2. Airchelon's build order (deliberately different from Every's)

Every built media → software → consulting, because it started as an AI-tools company with in-house engineering from day one. Airchelon starts as an editorial and expertise brand serving regulated industries — audiences that buy advisory and training readily, and software slowly. So the sequence should front-load the highest-leverage, lowest-build-cost line:

| Phase | Timing | What launches | Why this order |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0 — Media** | Year 1, month 1 | Essays + podcast, free + paid tiers | Needs to exist before anything else can be sold off the back of it |
| **1 — Community & live** | Year 1, once there's a real audience | Camps, demo days, webinars — bundled into paid tier at no extra charge | Builds the trust and direct relationships that consulting leads come from |
| **2 — Enterprise consulting/training** | Year 1–2 | Advisory and training engagements for regulated-industry clients | No product dependency; Every proved this is the fastest-growing, highest-leverage line once credibility exists |
| **3 — Cohort courses** | Year 2 | Paid, scheduled cohort-based courses | Packages repeatable consulting/training content into a scalable, lower-touch async format |
| **4 — Software instruments** | Year 2–3 | The "insight → instrument" pipeline matures into shipped tools, bundled into the subscription | Longest lead time; raise subscription price only once this adds real bundle value, mirroring Every's 2020→2025 44% increase |

## 3. Pricing by phase

**Free tier (all phases)**
- 1–2 free essays per month as a taster, full free public podcast (growth/top-of-funnel, same role it plays for Every's "AI & I")
- A free lightweight "state of the field" digest to build the top of the funnel

**Paid membership — Phase 0–1 (media + community)**
- **$15/month or $150/year** ($12.50/month effective, ~17% annual discount — same discount ratio Every uses)
- Includes: full essay archive, ad-free reading, live camps/demo days/webinars, community access
- Priced below Every's mature $288/year deliberately — there's no software in the bundle yet, so the price should reflect that plainly rather than starting inflated

**Paid membership — Phase 4 (once software instruments exist)**
- Raise to **$25/month or $240/year** — a ~60% increase justified specifically by the software addition, echoing Every's own precedent of raising price only when bundle value visibly increases
- Optional higher tier once there are 2+ tools and a real community, mirroring Every's "All Access": unlimited tool usage + partner credits + exclusive programming, priced at a premium (~$400–500/year)

**Enterprise consulting/training — Phase 2**
- Scoped engagements: **$15K–75K** depending on scope (compliance audit + workshop + advisory package)
- Or a retainer model: **$5K–10K/month** ongoing advisory for larger regulated-industry clients
- This is the line to prioritize building a sales motion around early, given Every's own trajectory

**Cohort courses — Phase 3**
- **$400–600 per course**, with a 20% discount for paid subscribers (consistent with Every's subscriber-discount pattern on courses)

## 4. Content production structure: founder + agents, not a writer roster

Airchelon's editorial output is produced by the founder directing a team of AI agents (research, drafting, repurposing, disclosure-flagging), not by a roster of independent contributor-writers the way Every operates. This changes the compensation question entirely: **there's no revenue-share model to design at launch, because there's no one to share revenue with yet.** Every's "marginal churn contribution" system exists to fairly split subscription revenue across multiple bylines — with a single founder voice, that mechanism doesn't apply.

What this structure requires instead, defined in full in the Editorial Operating System doc:
- A mandatory human review gate on every AI-drafted piece before publish (non-negotiable, risk-tiered by how much a piece could function as regulated-industry guidance)
- Disclosure of AI involvement where required by FTC guidance and EU AI Act Article 50

**When Airchelon eventually hires human writers**, the compensation question re-opens — but only then, and per-writer rather than as a blanket policy:
- New hires start on a flat per-piece rate (e.g., $300–500/essay), the same bootstrap terms Every itself uses before there's attribution data
- Individual writers transition to Every's revenue-share/churn-attribution model once there's enough subscriber data on their specific byline to measure it
- Writer-friendly terms worth keeping from Every: a departing writer retains a copy of the subscriber list they built, and can negotiate an individualized minimum for income stability while building an audience ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2021/01/26/everything-bundle-substack-newsletters))

Hiring triggers (capacity vs. credibility-gap) and the managing-editor role that appears at scale are specified in the Editorial Operating System doc, Section 7 — this doc doesn't duplicate that, it just confirms the financial mechanics slot in cleanly once that point is reached.

## 5. Realistic Year 1–2 targets

Grounding expectations in Every's actual pace rather than best-case assumptions:

- Typical newsletter free-to-paid conversion sits in the **2–5% range** — plan subscriber-revenue targets off of total free readership using that ratio, not off vanity free-subscriber counts.
- Every took **five years** to reach a diversified ~$7–10M revenue business. Airchelon's Year 1–2 goal should be building genuine free readership and a first credible consulting engagement or two — not replicating Every's current bundle economics immediately.
- The clearest, fastest path to meaningful revenue is **not** the subscription line — it's landing 2–3 enterprise consulting/training engagements once there's enough published work to demonstrate expertise. Treat that as the priority hire/business-development motion in Year 1, even before software exists.

## 6. Open decisions

- Currency and target market: price in USD to match Every's positioning and Airchelon's global remit, or lead with GBP given the London base? (Every prices globally in USD regardless of subscriber location.)
- Should software instruments (Phase 4) ever be sold standalone to non-subscribers, given the B2B regulated-industry audience may want a single tool without the full media bundle? Every doesn't do this; Airchelon's audience composition may warrant reconsidering it.
- Who owns the first consulting engagements operationally — is this a service line Airchelon staffs directly, or a referral/partner model in the early phase?
